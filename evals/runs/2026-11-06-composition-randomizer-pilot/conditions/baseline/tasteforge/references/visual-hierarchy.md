# Visual hierarchy

Make the first useful action or piece of information easy to find. Hierarchy is relative: if every region is bold, saturated, shadowed, and boxed, none stands out. Choose a primary focal area, supporting content, and quieter context based on the task.

In a payment form, the amount, recipient, and confirmation action matter more than a promotional illustration. In a publication, the story lead can dominate while navigation remains discoverable. In an operations dashboard, exceptions and comparable values may take priority over a large greeting.

Use alignment, proximity, scale, weight, contrast, and whitespace together. Related content should appear related before adding a container. Keep heading levels semantic even when visual sizes vary.

Poor: six equal statistic cards with no units or decision context. Improved: show the urgent exception first, provide its period and source, and place comparable supporting values in an aligned table. Poor: all buttons use the accent fill. Improved: distinguish the primary task from secondary and destructive actions with clear labels and consistent styling.

Review a brief scan of the actual content: can a reader identify the purpose, current state, next action, and supporting evidence? A source count of headings, colors, or cards cannot answer that alone.
