# Task structures and layout archetypes

These are original planning heuristics for matching information relationships to work. They are not aesthetic styles, validated universal solutions, or imported third-party templates. Combine/adapt only when the task justifies it. Preserve an established system during refinement.

## Quick routing

| User work | Useful starting structures | Decisions to keep together |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enter details and commit a consequential action | Focused task with check-answers; continuous workbench | Input, consequence/cost, recipient/object, explicit confirmation |
| Complete complex or branching input | Guided sequence | Current question, necessary context, progress, saved answers |
| Compare records or options | Comparison matrix; browse/filter/compare | Shared attributes, units, differences, selection criteria |
| Inspect and act on many records | Primary-detail workspace; triage queue | Selected record, surrounding list, filters, next action |
| Decide whether and which product to buy | Product decision rail; comparison sheet | Product evidence, fit, options, availability, total conditions |
| Understand a case study or article | Evidence-led editorial; document with outline | Claim, supporting artifact, explanation, navigation |
| Learn and practice | Reading/practice workspace | Instruction, example, question, explanatory feedback |
| Book an available slot | Availability-first booking | Date/time zone, duration, location, availability, confirmation |

## Focused task with check-answers

**Use:** a short or moderately sized transaction whose details should be reviewed before commitment.

**Anatomy:** concise task heading → grouped inputs → review of relevant values/consequences with specific change actions → explicit commit → confirmation or recoverable error. The review is a working summary, not another marketing introduction.

**Narrow:** one logical reading sequence; long names and values wrap without losing labels. Editing preserves values and returns to the review point. **Tradeoff:** a separate review adds navigation; justify it through consequence/error risk rather than habit. See the GOV.UK source pointers for concrete behavior guidance.

## Continuous workbench

**Use:** users repeatedly adjust a small set of inputs and need to see consequences immediately, such as a quote or transfer calculation.

**Anatomy:** editable working region beside or above a live consequence ledger; related costs/units remain adjacent; commitment is clearly separated from adjustment. Review/consent is explicit even if it occurs on the same page.

**Narrow:** inputs → immediately associated results → action; keep the decisive total and the selected object visible near commitment. **Tradeoff:** simultaneous editing and committing can create accidental actions; make the state and label unambiguous. A decorative left headline plus a form card is not a workbench unless the headline region actually helps a decision.

## Guided sequence

**Use:** branching eligibility, dependencies, or large forms that benefit from focused stages.

**Anatomy:** current decision and context → validation → next relevant question → review → outcome; progress reflects actual work, not a decorative three-step badge.

**Narrow:** preserve answers, allow relevant backward edits, and restore a sensible focus point. **Tradeoff:** extra navigation harms simple tasks. Do not turn two obvious fields into a wizard solely to create a different layout.

## Comparison matrix

**Use:** choices depend on shared attributes and differences.

**Anatomy:** criteria/constraints → aligned options and attribute rows → explicit selection or short comparison summary → action. Units, missing values, and provenance remain understandable.

**Narrow:** choose a deliberate attribute-first or option-detail route; purposeful table scrolling can be appropriate. **Tradeoff:** avoid a wall of low-value specifications. A pile of identical cards can destroy comparison even when every value is present.

## Primary-detail workspace

**Use:** people move between records and inspect/edit one while retaining list context.

**Anatomy:** scoped navigation/filter controls → comparable list/table → selected record details/actions. Selection, active filters, and return position persist.

**Narrow:** show list or detail with an explicit return path; retain filter and selection context rather than compressing two unusable panes. **Tradeoff:** not needed for a single isolated task. A side panel is useful only when the relationship to the list is clear.

## Triage queue

**Use:** operators must decide what needs attention first.

**Anatomy:** queue/exception priority → evidence and freshness → investigation/action → recorded outcome or next item. Summary indicators are supporting navigation into work, not the whole dashboard.

**Narrow:** keep the urgent item and its action reachable; move secondary analytics behind disclosure. **Tradeoff:** prioritization needs real domain rules and uncertainty handling. Do not invent urgency, health signals, or impressive metrics.

## Product decision rail or comparison sheet

**Use:** product evaluation depends on evidence, fit, options, and purchase conditions.

**Anatomy:** identifying media with accurate captions → decisive specifications/use → options/availability → price, shipping, and commitment → supporting care/returns/material evidence. A bounded purchase region can sit beside media on wide screens; a comparison sheet may be better when differences between variants dominate.

**Narrow:** show enough product evidence for trust without letting a decorative image hide every decision. Keep option selection and cost consequences adjacent; a persistent action must not obscure detail or keyboard focus. **Tradeoff:** a familiar image/buy split may be the right answer, but its content priorities must explain the product rather than fill a generic two-column shell.

## Evidence-led editorial

**Use:** readers evaluate a project, argument, or body of work.

**Anatomy:** concrete question/claim → relevant artifact or example → interpretation/tradeoffs → supporting detail and navigation/contact. Let the strongest evidence earn visual emphasis.

**Narrow:** maintain the claim/evidence/caption relationship. **Tradeoff:** art direction is valuable, but an enormous slogan followed by disconnected feature tiles can obscure the actual work. Treat fictional/sample outcomes honestly.

## Reading/practice workspace

**Use:** learning requires understanding an explanation and acting on it.

**Anatomy:** learning goal → concise instruction/example → question or exercise → specific feedback → next step; optional glossary/tools remain nearby but secondary.

**Narrow:** reading measure and task order survive reflow; a supporting pane becomes deliberate disclosure rather than an interleaved distraction. **Tradeoff:** persistent sidebars compete with reading. Engagement decoration should not replace understandable progress or feedback.

## Browse/filter/compare

**Use:** users need to discover a suitable item from many candidates.

**Anatomy:** meaningful search/filter scope → results with decision-relevant attributes → selection/compare state → item or task. Preserve criteria and place applied-filter feedback close to results.

**Narrow:** filters can move into an accessible disclosure, with active criteria still visible. **Tradeoff:** choosing tiles, a table, or a list depends on what must be compared; do not default to an equal card grid merely because the content repeats.

## Document with outline

**Use:** readers need both a long explanation and a stable sense of location.

**Anatomy:** document context/title → navigable outline → bounded reading column → related material or next task. Examples sit beside their explanation where practical.

**Narrow:** the outline becomes reachable navigation and headings retain meaningful anchors. **Tradeoff:** navigation chrome should not consume more space than the document or conceal the requested answer.

## Availability-first booking

**Use:** a feasible time/date is the principal constraint.

**Anatomy:** appointment/service context → date/time zone/duration → available options → required details → review/confirmation; no-times-available and changed-availability states have useful exits.

**Narrow:** maintain time-zone and selected-slot context when moving between dates and details. **Tradeoff:** a large calendar is not always needed; a short date/slot list may serve limited availability better.

## What constitutes an alternative?

Show the region map, information order, navigation, state sequence, and narrow behavior. Two candidates with the same task relationships but different fonts, color themes, corner radii, or decorative grid spans are the same structural approach. Conversely, changing a familiar interaction merely to appear novel is not evidence of better UX. Select for the real work, then apply visual variation within that structure.
