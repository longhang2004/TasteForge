## What transfers into TasteForge

The [skill reference](../skills/tasteforge/references/effects-and-composition.md) maps twelve technique families to purpose, task fit and a usable static state. Effect selection follows the selected layout; it does not randomize navigation, review semantics or operational evidence. Existing motion/accessibility guidance still applies.

The [local effects lab](../examples/effects-lab/README.md) contains original implementations of a one-shot text emphasis, a position transition between list/grid layouts, and a native-range image comparison. Its vector illustration is original repo-native SVG. No third-party implementation, runtime, image or AI prompt is copied. These are mechanism studies, not complete product templates or new benchmark samples.

The adaptation is deliberate: decorative demos can loop to advertise an effect; a product can use a bounded user-triggered treatment and retain a useful rest state. A source can teach clipping or continuity while its copy, timing, semantics, asset choices and dependencies remain unsuitable for a particular task.

## Evidence and open questions

The supplied Colorion marker and masked-reveal pages were rendered in isolated Chromium 156.0.8078.4. Reduced and normal media preferences were sampled; computed duration/iteration as well as names must be considered before inferring whether an animation runs. This inspection covers two exact pages, not all 123 effects, every browser, keyboard behaviour or contrast throughout animation.

The [recorded sampling](../examples/effects-lab/evidence/colorion-observations.json) found the marker's normal 3s infinite animation reduced to a single 0.00001s iteration; its animation name remained set. Masked-reveal letters changed from a 4.4s infinite animation to no named animation under reduced motion. These observations support those two implementations' preference handling, not a certification of the entire collection.

Local lab captures/checks are recorded with its example. They check named interactions, static/reduced-motion behaviour and narrow layouts. They do not establish better comprehension, performance on target hardware, accessibility conformance or a general reduction in generic design. Those require task-specific evaluation and, where relevant, user feedback.
